TCFD &amp; CONI Content Analysis of 2025 Sustainability Reports (BNI, BCA, Mandiri, BRI)

# Project background

Banks are central to the climate transition. Their own operations produce few emissions, but the loans and investments they finance produce far more, and investors and regulators increasingly expect banks to show how they identify, manage and measure climate risk. The TCFD recommendations became the common structure for this reporting, and they have since been built into the IFRS S2 climate standard issued by the ISSB. Indonesian banks report through integrated sustainability reports, not stand-alone TCFD reports, so the quality of their climate disclosure is not obvious from a quick read. This project asks how detailed and how quantified the climate disclosure of four large Indonesian banks really is, and where it falls short.

Research questions
RQ1. How deeply does each bank disclose each of the 11 TCFD sub-categories?
RQ2. Which areas are strongest and which are weakest across the four banks?
RQ3. Do the content-analysis scores line up with external ratings (S&P Global, CDP) and with quantitative indicators such as sustainable financing and emissions?

# Method origin
The scoring approach adapts the CONI model (Beck, Campbell and Shrives, 2010) as applied to TCFD reports by Xhindole, Tarquinio and Sierra-Garcia (2025), who compared listed companies in Italy and Spain. The same 11 sub-categories and the same 0 to 5 depth scale are used here for banks in Indonesia.
Data and workbook structure

The workbook is Climate-disclosure-quality-of-four-Indonesian-banks.xlsx.
1. Summary : Score table (11 areas x 4 banks), total score out of 55, disclosure completeness (%), area averages, colour key and four charts
2. TCFD_Content Analysis : One row per TCFD sub-category (GOV-1 to MET-3). For each bank: the evidence text with report page references, and the CONI score
3. CSA & CDP: S&P Global CSA and ESG scores, CDP climate, forest and water scores, with source links
4. Benchmark_Summary	:	Sustainable financing, green portfolio, sustainability-linked loans, bonds, change in Scope 1 and 2 emissions, total operating emissions and financed emissions
5. Operating : Scope 1 and Scope 2 by bank and year (2023 to 2025), index 2023 = 100, change vs 2023, source page for every value
6. Financed : Financed (Scope 3) emissions by bank and year, converted to million tCO2e, with loan-portfolio coverage and source page
7. Chart_Data	: Formula-driven tables that feed the emissions charts

Scoring scale
Score	Meaning
0	: Not disclosed
1 to 2	: General narrative (1) or detailed narrative (2)
3 to 4	: Narrative with numbers. Type 3 (numbers only) did not occur; 4 is narrative with numbers
5	:Narra tive, numbers and comparison over time

Scoring rule used. The scores follow a strict rule so that each point can be defended from the evidence:
•	A score of 4 needs a numeric result, KPI weight or target in that sub-category's own evidence. Meeting frequency and coverage ratios alone do not count.
•	A score of 5 needs a comparison with an earlier period.
•	For targets (MET-3), a 5 needs quantified GHG targets with performance reported.
Derived indicators. Total score is the sum of the 11 scores (maximum 55). Disclosure completeness is the total divided by 55.

Evidence convention
Every score in TCFD_Content Analysis is backed by one to five numbered evidence points, each with a page reference to the source report. This lets a reviewer check any score against the original document.

# Key findings
Overall scores
BRI scores highest with 43 of 55 points, which is 78.2% disclosure completeness. Mandiri follows with 39 points (70.9%), then BNI with 37 points (67.3%), and BCA is last with 32 points (58.2%). The four-bank average is 37.75 points, or 68.6%.

# Strongest and weakest pillars
Disclosure is deepest on metrics and targets, which average 4.92 out of 5. Every bank scores 5 on climate metrics and on Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions, and three of the four score 5 on targets. Strategy averages 3.33: all four banks score 4 on impact on business and finance, but the detail on time horizons and on resilience under scenarios varies. Governance averages 2.75, with board oversight at 3.0 and management's role at 2.5. Risk management is weakest at 2.50, and managing climate risk and integrating it into overall risk management score only 2 at every bank.

# What separates the banks
BRI leads because it combines strong governance (4 on both governance sub-categories) with disclosure the others lack. It is the only bank with a numeric sector materiality assessment, in which the top three loan sectors make up 77.98% of the portfolio, and the only bank that discloses an internal carbon price. BCA trails because it gives no quantified greenhouse gas target, no scenario results and no assessment outputs. Mandiri and BRI publish KPI weights for directors and managers, BNI, Mandiri and BRI report numeric stress-test results, and every bank except BCA sets quantified emissions targets.All four banks describe their climate risk processes in detail but, in the pages checked, none discloses numeric limits or thresholds for managing and integrating climate risk. This is the common gap in the sample.

# Emissions

Financed emissions dominate the footprint. They are between 96% and 99.6% of each bank's reported emissions, so targets that cover operations alone address only a small part of the total. Operating emissions (Scope 1 and 2) in 2025 were 176,615 tonnes of CO2 equivalent at BNI, 153,883 at BCA, 243,736 at Mandiri and 445,140 at BRI. Against 2023, BNI fell 6.7%, Mandiri fell 17.6% and BRI fell 8.5%, while BCA rose 8.5%. BCA is the only bank above its 2023 level, and its 2025 figure is annualised from January to September data.

Financed emissions in 2025 were 44.64 million tonnes at BNI, 20.3 million at Mandiri and 12.37 million at BRI. BCA has not reported a 2025 figure; its latest is 37.43 million tonnes for 2024. These figures are not like-for-like because coverage changed. The share of the loan portfolio covered rose from 70.3% to 90.5% at BNI in 2025, from 50% to 100% at BCA in 2024, and from 44.4% to 60.9% at Mandiri over 2023 to 2025. Part of each reported increase therefore reflects wider coverage, so trends should be compared, not levels.

# Sustainable financing

Total sustainable financing was Rp196.7 trillion at BNI, Rp255.38 trillion at BCA, Rp315.8 trillion at Mandiri and Rp811.9 trillion at BRI. As a share of total loans, that is 22.3% at BNI, 25.8% at BCA, 21.1% at Mandiri and 60.4% at BRI. BRI's much higher share reflects how it counts its MSME loan book as social lending, so shares should be compared with care. The green portfolio was Rp78.0 trillion at BNI, Rp113.08 trillion at BCA, Rp166.2 trillion at Mandiri and Rp93.2 trillion at BRI. Sustainability-linked loans were Rp6.6 trillion at BNI, Rp2.6 trillion at BCA and Rp4.11 trillion at Mandiri, with no figure disclosed by BRI. Only BNI and Mandiri disclosed bond issuance, at Rp5.0 trillion each.

# External ratings

External ratings and content scores do not line up. BRI is first on both measures, with S&P CSA and ESG scores of 74 and 74. BCA ranks second on S&P (62 and 62) but last on content analysis. Mandiri has S&P scores of 54 (CSA) and 55 (ESG) and ranks second here. BNI is last on S&P (39 and 42) but third here. The instruments measure different things: S&P covers broad ESG performance, while this analysis measures the depth of TCFD-aligned climate disclosure in the report. On CDP, only Mandiri disclosed, with a B for climate and a B for forests, while BNI, BCA and BRI did not disclose.

# Recommendations
# For banks

Banks should first quantify climate risk impact and scenario results. Reporting probability of default, expected credit loss and capital effects by scenario, and comparing them with the previous year, would address the clearest gap, especially for BCA.

They should also disclose KPI weights and results for the board and management, because linking pay to climate indicators is only verifiable when weights, targets and outcomes are published. Risk management needs numbers too: exposure limits, thresholds or risk appetite metrics for climate-sensitive sectors, and a visible link into overall risk management. That is the weakest area at all four banks.

On targets, banks should set quantified interim targets for financed emissions. Sector pathways and external validation of targets, as at BRI, make them testable. Financed emissions should be reported on a consistent and timely basis, with the current-year figure, stated coverage and stable boundaries between years. Operational Scope 3 should extend beyond business travel or purchased goods to the other relevant categories. Finally, banks should prepare for IFRS S2 by organising the climate chapter around the standard's four pillars, as Mandiri already does.

# For the research

The study would be stronger with a second coder and a reported inter-coder agreement measure such as Cohen's kappa. A sensitivity sheet should be added, because the ranking depends on the scoring rule. Under the looser rule in the source paper, where meeting frequency and coverage ratios count as numbers, totals shift to 40 for BNI, 42 for BCA, 43 for Mandiri and 45 for BRI, so BCA's last place is rule-dependent while BRI and Mandiri stay in the top two. The sample could be extended to more banks and to the 2023 and 2024 reports to test whether disclosure is improving, and annual reports and websites could be checked to see whether they change scores. Links with external ratings should be tested on a larger sample, since four banks cannot support statistical inference.
